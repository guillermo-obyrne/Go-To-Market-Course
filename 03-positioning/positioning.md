## 1. Positioning framework

- **For (target audience):** Whoever is organizing a group booking and fronting the money for it, for whom a booking that falls through costs them socially, not just financially.
- **Who want / need:** To book a group trip without paying for everyone out of pocket and hoping to get paid back.
- **We offer:** A way to book a curated experience as a group from Roamly's vetted hosts, with one link per guest.
- **Because of (value proposition):** No chasing, because guests pay directly and the organizer stops being the collector and the booking confirms when the group has paid.
- **Despite (barriers):** Despite other competitors offering the most robust social planning features, full-holiday expense tracking and the plan for the whole weekend, while Roamly Groups covers a single booking.
- **Better than (differentiation):** Better than Batch: Roamly's 12,000+ vetted hosts, with repeat bookings at three times the industry average, make it less likely a bad listing lands on the organizer. Batch sells featured placement to vendors.

## 2. Positioning statement

### First draft
Roamly Groups lets milestone trip organizers book a vetted host's experience for a celebration, with one link per guest. Everyone pays their own share, and the booking only confirms once the group has paid, so you never front the bill or lose face.

### Pressure test
- **Test I ran:** B · Competitor discomfort
- **What it revealed:** The test showed my first draft was built on two things Batch can already say: an audience label ("milestone") that is Batch's own, and a mechanism (each guest pays their own share) that Batch could ship. Naming "milestone trip organizers" also describes an occasion rather than the person and their problem, so it narrows the segment without sharpening it. The audience should be abstracted to a person with a problem: someone who organizes for a group and fronts the money.

  The one thing I would defend against Batch is the vetted network of hosts. Batch's hosts can pay for featured placement, while Roamly has 12,000+ vetted hosts and repeat bookings at three times the industry average. The first draft barely used that advantage, so it needs its own clause.

  "Lose face" was the wrong promise for the statement. A trip can still fall through if an invitee doesn't pay, and the booking mechanism doesn't prevent that. The social cost is still real when a high-friction booking experience collapses, so it stays in the audience definition, but the statement should promise only what the mechanism delivers. The risk Roamly Groups removes is the financial one, which the organizer previously absorbed entirely: the booking only confirms once the group has paid, so each invitee absorbs their own cost.

### Final statement
Roamly Groups lets whoever is organizing a group book from hosts Roamly has vetted, with one link per guest. Everyone pays their own share, and the booking only confirms once the group has paid, so the organizer never fronts the money.
