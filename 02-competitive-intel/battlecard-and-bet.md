## 2. Battlecard

### 1. Know the Landscape

#### Competitor overview
Batch is a free group-planning app with a party marketplace attached. It serves organizers aged 23–35 planning milestone weekends and birthdays, and it comes from bachelorette parties, where it still calls itself #1. It positions itself two ways: to organizers, as the one app for the whole trip; to vendors, as a marketing engine rather than a middleman. Its scale figures are self-reported and contradict each other (3M+ and 5M+ users on its supplier pages; city counts from "over two dozen" to 70+ depending on the page or listing).

#### Key offerings
- Planning tools: guest roles (Guest of Honor, Planner, Guest), polls, a shared itinerary, group chat, and an expense ledger.
- Marketplace: experiences, stays, food and drink, boats, party buses, live shows, party services.
- Bookings are confirmed instantly, confirmed manually by the vendor, or handed off to the vendor's own site.
- "Buy My Ticket": per Batch's FAQ, guests can buy their own additional ticket from a link on an itinerary event. **[Author's decision, not fully vetted against Batch documentation]:** it covers extra per-person experiences within the weekend. The main booking with the host is split between guests via Venmo and tracked in the ledger.
- Batch does not move money between guests. The ledger records what people paid each other in Venmo or elsewhere.

#### Pricing
- Organizers: free. No fees on top of vendor prices, price match, card held until the vendor confirms. Listings are priced per person. Each vendor picks one of three cancellation policies, including one with no refunds at all.
- Vendors: monthly or annual subscription, sold as zero commission (though a commission can apply), with plan prices not published. Vendors can also pay to upgrade for more visibility and promotion ("Upgrade for Growth"; featured placement on paid plans).

### 2. Understand the Battlefield

#### Key strengths
- They reach the organizer first. Batch is chosen when someone decides "we're doing a bachelorette," before any experience is picked. Roamly Groups only comes into play once a Roamly experience is already on the table.
- They cover the whole occasion. Lodging, dinner, bars and activities sit in one plan. Roamly Groups covers one booking.
- Their tools are built for the occasion. Guest of Honor roles and secret events hidden from the guest of honor are celebration features, not generic travel features.
- They're hard to undercut on price. Free, no added fees, and price match.
- Vendors like their economics. Subscription instead of commission, a direct-traffic option, and integrations with FareHarbor, Peek and Singenuity.
- Guests already have a self-service step. Buy My Ticket lets guests buy their own additional ticket for experiences sold per person within the weekend **[author's decision, not fully vetted]**. The main booking is not split this way. A guest who skips a ticketed event just doesn't attend; the rest of the booking holds.

#### Key weaknesses
- The organizer still carries shared costs. Anything not sold as a per-person ticket gets settled through a ledger and Venmo after someone has paid.
- Batch is often not part of the transaction. On direct-traffic listings, the vendor owns the booking, so Batch can't guarantee fulfilment, hold a slot, or handle refunds.
- Paid placement sits next to curation. Vendors can buy featured placement; Batch describes its ranking as relevance-driven and does not say whether paid upgrades affect rank. Individual App Store reviews describe a fraudulent drag-show listing and a refund the organizer couldn't recover. That's anecdote, not a pattern, but it's an opening.
- Cancellation terms vary by vendor. Under the strict policy, a guest who drops out costs someone the full price.
- The brand is narrow. Party weekends in US party cities, aged 23–35. Reunions, multi-generational birthdays and celebrations built around a cultural activity sit outside it.

### 3. Win the Deal

#### Why you win
- Each guest pays their own share before the booking is confirmed. On its own, this feature is not defensible: Batch already pays vendors out through Stripe (for Instant Booking and Manual Confirmation) and could build it. What is defensible is structural. To collect shares, hold the slot and enforce a payment cutoff, you have to own the transaction. Batch's vendor pitch is that it doesn't. Copying the feature fully would contradict its own business model.
- Trust in the experience. 12,000+ vetted hosts and repeat bookings at three times the industry average (Final Project Brief). When a celebration goes wrong, the organizer is the one who answers for it. Batch sells featured placement to vendors; Roamly vets its hosts.
- The segment is already ours. These organizers are active Roamly users who tried to do this on Roamly and failed. Batch has to acquire them; we have to stop losing them.
- Reach. Four continents vs US party cities. A destination reunion in Lisbon is not a Batch use case.

#### Why they win
- The organizer plans the whole weekend. Roamly Groups is one booking in a plan Batch already holds. We are a line item in their itinerary.
- Bachelorettes in Nashville, Vegas or Scottsdale. That's Batch's home turf, and Roamly's 40 cities and its curated categories may not cover party-bus-and-drag-brunch demand at all.
- Price. If Roamly Groups adds any fee, Batch wins that comparison outright.
- Lock-in. Once a group has joined Batch, moving them is expensive. One reviewer who hated the app still stayed because she'd already gotten 14 people to download it.
- Our value proposition may solve the smaller pain for this audience. For a 25-year-old's bachelorette, Venmo is normal, and Buy My Ticket lets guests buy their own ticket for per-person events **[author's decision, not fully vetted]**. For this group, coordination may hurt more than payment does.
- Vendor economics. If Roamly charges hosts a commission, a host listed on both platforms has a reason to push groups toward Batch's direct traffic.

#### Why you
Why choose Roamly over doing nothing: on Roamly, nobody fronts the bill. Every guest confirms and pays their own share for a vetted experience, and the booking only goes through once the group has paid.

### 4. Handle the Hard Moments

#### Quick dismiss
"Batch keeps track of who owes what. Roamly Groups collects it before anyone pays for everyone."

#### Landmine questions
Ask the organizer these. Roamly has to be able to answer each one about itself first.

- "On your last group booking, who paid upfront, and how long did it take to get everyone's share back?"
- "If a guest drops out a week before, who absorbs the cost?"
- "Did you book inside the app, or get sent to the vendor's website? Who do you call if it goes wrong?"
- "Do you know whether listings are ranked on quality or on what vendors pay to be featured?"
- "Is everyone in your group up for a party weekend, or are parents and old friends coming too?"

#### Objection handling
- "We've already planned everything in Batch." → Don't fight for the whole trip. Roamly Groups is one link the organizer drops into their Batch itinerary, where each guest pays their own share. Coexisting may be the right strategy, not a fallback.
- "Venmo works fine." → It works once someone has already paid for everyone. If your group always pays back within a day, you may not need us. Be honest; that group isn't our segment.
- "Batch has no fees and matches prices." → No answer yet. Roamly Groups pricing is a Module 5 decision. Until then, this objection loses.
- "Guests can already buy their own ticket on Batch." → **[Author's decision, not fully vetted against Batch documentation.]** For per-person extras within a weekend, yes, and a guest who doesn't buy a ticketed event simply doesn't attend. The main booking with the host is still split through Venmo and the ledger, and Batch's About page says the app does not handle payments within the group. For a private group booking, ask who paid.
- "Do I have to get everyone onto another app?" → This is Roamly's landmine as much as Batch's. If guests must create an account to pay their share, the status quo wins. This is a product decision to settle before launch, not a messaging problem.

#### Gaps that limit this battlecard
- No evidence yet that our segment ever meets Batch. Support tickets, follow-ups after failed group bookings, and exit surveys are where to look.
- City overlap is unknown. We don't know how many of Roamly's 40 cities are among Batch's party cities.
- Roamly's pricing and host commission are undecided (Module 5), so the price and vendor-economics comparisons can't be settled yet.
- We don't know whether guests need a Roamly account to pay their share.
- Buy My Ticket's scope (per-person extras, not the main booking) is the author's decision and is not confirmed in Batch documentation; worth testing the flow. Other Batch claims were checked against Batch's pages by a second AI on 16 November 2026; spot-check the quotes before relying on them.

## 4. Strategic bet

### Data
- **Who Roamly Groups is for:** I'm doubling down on the Module 1 segment: active Roamly travelers who have tried to book a celebration as a group, served through the organizer. The battlecard shows Batch targets the same organizer and occasions, so the segment is real and contested. Within it, I'm aiming at celebrations beyond the party weekend, such as reunions and multi-generational birthdays.
- **The one thing worth choosing it for:** Trust in the experience: 12,000+ vetted hosts and three times the industry repeat rate (Brief). When a celebration goes wrong, the organizer answers for it, and a vetted host lowers that risk. Individual Batch reviews describe a fraudulent listing.
- **The biggest threat to that position:** Batch owns the customer at intent, which is huge for brand recognition, and in the bachelorette niche Batch is the status quo. Organizers may prefer one free all-in-one app over a trust advantage, especially if Batch closes the payment gap.

### Insight
Batch's strength depends on getting the whole group onto its app. That is a cost to guests, and Roamly would add the same cost if it required accounts to pay, so Roamly Groups must let guests pay without downloading anything.

### Belief
I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when an organizer is planning a high-stakes celebration for people they care about, because vetted experiences give peace of mind in a high-stress, high-attendance, complex booking, and guests can join without friction.

### Bet
- **I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by:** letting organizers book without fronting the bill: each guest pays their own share through a guest checkout link with no app to download, and the booking holds once the group has paid. *Open product decision, not yet made: whether guests create a quick account without a download, or use guest checkout without a permanent account.*
- **To make this bet, I am deprioritizing:** post-booking expense tracking: a ledger for rentals, bars and restaurants. Roamly sells experiences, so Batch's whole-weekend ledger isn't ours to match. It is separate from the split-payment promise, which is collecting each guest's share before the booking is confirmed.
